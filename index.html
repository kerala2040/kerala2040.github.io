Independent research · Kerala electricity system

Kerala's power system
under stress

Kerala used 29.31 TWh of electricity in FY2024–25. But annual energy totals alone cannot describe what happens during a peak-demand hour. Kerala2040 examines demand timing, hydropower scheduling, electricity imports and transmission constraints using official records and explicitly bounded models.

Main research question

Why is Kerala susceptible to electricity shortages and peak-hour supply restrictions, and what structural and operational factors determine whether the system can meet demand under stress?

The analysis is ongoing and has not been submitted for publication. Measured hourly demand, dispatch and interstate interchange data are needed to test the models against observed operation.

Where the project stands

What has been modelled, and what is still missing

The project has a 2024–25 hourly demand reconstruction, chronological adequacy runs, hydropower-timing experiments and a public-data transmission screen. The hourly demand profile is not measured telemetry, so reported shortage magnitudes must be read as scenario outputs.

Historical data

354 daily records, one reconstructed hourly year

Official annual and daily electricity evidence has been reconciled, with 354 observed FY2024–25 daily totals preserved exactly in the current hourly reconstruction.

Scenario results

Hydro timing, imports and line-loading screens

Chronological adequacy, hydro-timing sensitivities, interstate-transfer stress cases and public-network screening are documented with their assumptions and limitations. They have not been calibrated against complete operational records.

Missing data

Observed 15-minute or hourly demand

The current 8,760-hour demand series is reconstructed from daily energy, sparse SLDC extrema and ERA5. Obtaining actual 15-minute/hourly Kerala demand—and ideally generation and interchange—is the highest-value next step.

Current synthesis

Why annual energy does not settle hourly adequacy

The same annual energy can produce different adequacy outcomes when peak demand, hydro output and import limits occur at different hours. The screening experiments test these interactions; they cannot attribute a specific 2026 outage to any one mechanism.

New result · 6 October 2026

Weather improves observed daily-demand models in six of seven XGBoost tests

The October 2026 experiment uses 353 observed daily SLDC electricity-consumption values matched with complete local-day ERA5 reanalysis. Seven expanding-window monthly holdouts compare calendar-only models against independently tuned models that also use weather. This analysis does not train on the reconstructed hourly load.

Boundary

Same-day ERA5 is not a day-ahead forecast

The models use same-day realized ERA5 reanalysis from one financial year. The result supports a robust within-year weather–demand relationship; it does not establish causality, day-ahead forecasting performance or multi-year generalization.

Outstanding work

The evidence needed to test these findings against operation

1 · Replace or validate the reconstructed hourly load

Priority data: aggregated Kerala 15-minute/hourly demand for at least one complete year, preferably multiple years. This would enable genuine continuous-hour validation rather than validation only against sparse published extrema.

2 · Improve operational chronology

Interval internal generation—especially hydro—and scheduled/actual interstate drawal would let stressed-hour adequacy be tested against observed operation rather than daily-average replay.

3 · Propagate uncertainty

If measured interval load cannot be obtained, the paper will use an ensemble of admissible hourly reconstructions rather than treating one synthetic profile as exact.

4 · Strengthen spatial validation

Substation/transformer loading, current equipment parameters and additional independent planning evidence would improve the statewide network screen but cannot replace an operator-grade AC/N-1 study.

5 · Finish the literature positioning

The journal contribution must be established against Kerala transition studies, resource-adequacy work, hydro-rich/import-dependent systems, chronological adequacy and public-data grid reconstruction.

6 · Freeze and reproduce the paper results

Only after the stronger chronology and final sensitivity matrix are complete will the numerical results be frozen into a traceable publication manifest.

Scope and limitations

Screening results are not operational validation

Included in this study

Historical evidence and reproducible sensitivities

  • Source-audited historical accounting.
  • Bounded hourly demand reconstruction.
  • Chronological adequacy and transfer sensitivities.
  • Hydro timing experiments.
  • Public 110-kV+ network stress screening.
  • Independent corroboration where available.

Outside the study's scope

Not measured hourly demand, an operator-grade digital twin, AC/N-1 security study, probabilistic reliability certification, validated catchment/reservoir model, exact 2026 outage post-mortem, legal buildable-MW assessment or least-cost 2040 investment portfolio.
