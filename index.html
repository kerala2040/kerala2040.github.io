Kerala power-system stress · research programme

Why can the system run short
when annual energy can look adequate?

Kerala2040 is an independent, evidence-first investigation of the interaction between demand chronology, hydropower flexibility, external electricity and internal transmission deliverability in Kerala. The project is now being developed toward a journal paper rather than a conference deliverable.

Main research question

Why is Kerala susceptible to electricity shortages and peak-hour supply restrictions, and what structural and operational factors determine whether the system can meet demand under stress?

Current status: a credible paper-sized research question and audited screening evidence exist, but the study is not yet ready for journal submission. The main evidence gap is measured interval demand and related operational chronology.

Where the project stands

The diagnosis exists. The publication evidence is still being strengthened.

Kerala2040 has moved beyond an exploratory collection of models. The current phase is deliberately narrower: turn the strongest evidence into a reproducible, uncertainty-aware paper without overstating what public data can prove.

Established baseline

Historical accounting and chronology framework

Official annual and daily electricity evidence has been reconciled, with 354 observed FY2024–25 daily totals preserved exactly in the current hourly reconstruction.

Audited screening result

Hydro, transfer and network mechanisms

Chronological adequacy, hydro-timing sensitivities, interstate-transfer stress cases and public-network screening have all passed a conservative scientific QA with explicit claim boundaries.

Publication blocker

Measured interval chronology

The current 8,760-hour demand series is reconstructed from daily energy, sparse SLDC extrema and ERA5. Obtaining actual 15-minute/hourly Kerala demand—and ideally generation and interchange—is the highest-value next step.

Current synthesis

Stress appears to be an interaction problem, not a single annual-energy number.

The working hypothesis is that difficult hours emerge from several constraints acting together. This is a synthesis to test more rigorously, not an attribution of any specific 2026 outage.

New result · 6 October 2026

The weather signal survives rolling daily-demand tests—and temperature carries most of it.

A new observed-demand experiment uses 353 source-reported FY2024–25 SLDC daily-consumption records matched to complete local-day ERA5 weather. It is separate from the reconstructed 8,760-hour load proxy.

Weather helps in most independent months.

LightGBM improves in 5 of 7 rolling holdouts and XGBoost in 6 of 7. Pooled RMSE falls from 4.558 to 2.865 MU/day for XGBoost and from 4.657 to 2.903 MU/day for LightGBM.

Boundary

Retrospective association, not a forecast.

The models use same-day realized ERA5 reanalysis from one financial year. The result supports a robust within-year weather–demand relationship; it does not establish causality, day-ahead forecasting performance or multi-year generalization.

What the current evidence says

Five findings are strong enough to carry forward.

Observed

Demand has risen materially.

Official broad consumption rose from 22.54 TWh in FY2020–21 to 29.31 TWh in FY2024–25.

Evidence →
Reconstructed + observed daily ML

Chronology and weather both matter.

The hourly reconstruction still remains a proxy, but a separate observed-daily experiment now shows that ERA5 weather improves rolling out-of-sample demand prediction in most monthly holdouts, with temperature carrying the clearest signal.

Evidence →
Sensitivity

Hydro timing can be decisive.

At the 4,455 MW transfer reference, allowing the same daily hydro energy to move within the day reduces modeled unserved energy from about 375.0 GWh to 11.75 GWh in the reference sensitivity.

Evidence →
Official + sensitivity

Interstate transfer is a major adequacy lever.

CEA reports a 4,455 MW ATC reference. Modeled adequacy deteriorates substantially under transparent 80% and 60% transfer stress cases.

Evidence →
Robust screen

Internal geography also matters.

Twenty-one source-backed lines remain overloaded across all eight tested boundary-injection allocations, grouped into twelve robust corridor signals; the Shoranur area has independent CEA corroboration.

Evidence →
Boundary

No event-causal claim.

Kerala2040 does not yet prove why a particular 2026 restriction occurred. That would require interval demand, dispatch, interchange, outage and reservoir evidence for the event.

What we still need to figure out

The remaining work is targeted, not open-ended.

1 · Replace or validate the reconstructed hourly load

Priority data: aggregated Kerala 15-minute/hourly demand for at least one complete year, preferably multiple years. This would enable genuine continuous-hour validation rather than validation only against sparse published extrema.

2 · Improve operational chronology

Interval internal generation—especially hydro—and scheduled/actual interstate drawal would let stressed-hour adequacy be tested against observed operation rather than daily-average replay.

3 · Propagate uncertainty

If measured interval load cannot be obtained, the paper will use an ensemble of admissible hourly reconstructions rather than treating one synthetic profile as exact.

4 · Strengthen spatial validation

Substation/transformer loading, current equipment parameters and additional independent planning evidence would improve the statewide network screen without pretending it is operator-grade AC/N-1 analysis.

5 · Finish the literature positioning

The journal contribution must be established against Kerala transition studies, resource-adequacy work, hydro-rich/import-dependent systems, chronological adequacy and public-data grid reconstruction.

6 · Freeze and reproduce the paper results

Only after the stronger chronology and final sensitivity matrix are complete will the numerical results be frozen into a traceable publication manifest.

Final goal

A publishable diagnosis first; policy use second.

The first paper is intentionally narrower than “Kerala's optimal transition to 2040.” It aims to explain stressed-hour vulnerability before prescribing an investment portfolio.

Scientific boundary

Useful screening evidence is not an operator digital twin.

Defensible now

Public-data diagnostic and screening study

  • Source-audited historical accounting.
  • Bounded hourly demand reconstruction.
  • Chronological adequacy and transfer sensitivities.
  • Hydro timing experiments.
  • Public 110-kV+ network stress screening.
  • Independent corroboration where available.

Not claimed

Not measured hourly demand, an operator-grade digital twin, AC/N-1 security study, probabilistic reliability certification, validated catchment/reservoir model, exact 2026 outage post-mortem, legal buildable-MW assessment or least-cost 2040 investment portfolio.
