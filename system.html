Meet Kerala

A compact tropical state between mountains and sea.

Kerala lies on India's south-west coast between the Arabian Sea and the Western Ghats. Beaches, backwaters, densely settled lowlands, plantation highlands, reservoirs and tropical forests coexist within 38,863 km².

Landscape

The Western Ghats provide elevation, rainfall and river systems that support hydropower while also creating steep, ecologically sensitive and landslide-prone terrain.

People & culture

Malayalam-speaking Kerala has long Indian Ocean connections, migration and religious diversity alongside local traditions including Onam, boat races, Kathakali, Mohiniyattam and Theyyam.

Industrial geology

Around Chavara, monazite-bearing heavy-mineral sands occur with ilmenite, rutile, zircon and sillimanite. This is a regional mineral deposit—not a claim that Kerala's beaches are uniformly “thorium beaches.”

The electricity balance

Low per-person use does not mean a simple system.

Kerala's official broad consumption rose from 22.54 TWh in FY2020–21 to 29.31 TWh in FY2024–25. Electricity arriving from outside the state is structurally important, while hydropower provides much of the flexible in-state generation.

Accounting rule

Do not manufacture one import percentage.

Annual consumption, category sales, periphery input and reported import-energy totals have different official boundaries.

Imports are not automatically a failure.

Interconnection is normal and can be economically valuable. The vulnerability question is whether dependable external supply and transfer capability are available at the difficult hour.

Weather on both sides

The monsoon shapes supply and demand.

Heat and humidity influence cooling-related demand. Rainfall affects reservoir conditions and hydro opportunity. Cloud and wind affect variable renewable output. Extreme rain and landslides can become infrastructure hazards.
