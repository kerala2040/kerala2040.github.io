Publication roadmap

There is a paper here.
It is not finished yet.

The current repository contains enough audited evidence to justify a focused journal study. The remaining work is not “add everything possible”; it is to close the specific evidence gaps that could change the central conclusion, especially hourly demand and operational chronology.

Working concept

Beyond annual energy balance.

Working title

Beyond Annual Energy Balance: Diagnosing Peak-Hour Electricity Stress in Kerala through Demand Chronology, Hydropower Flexibility, Interstate Transfer and Network Deliverability.

Core question

Why can Kerala become vulnerable to electricity shortages and peak-hour restrictions even when annual supply-demand accounting appears adequate, and which temporal and spatial constraints dominate under stress?

Positioning: the first paper is a diagnosis paper, not a “100% renewable Kerala by 2040” pathway paper and not an event-by-event political attribution study.

Current evidence

What the paper can already build on.

Demand chronology

354 FY2024–25 SLDC daily energy totals are observed and preserved exactly. The 8,760-hour intraday shape is reconstructed from sparse published extrema, clock-time structure and ERA5 weather. Held-out extrema RMSE is 155.2 MW versus 162.0 MW for the strong static reconstruction. This is shape validation conditional on known daily energy, not measured-hour validation.

New · within-year robustness passed

Observed daily weather–demand relationship

A separate experiment uses 353 observed SLDC daily-consumption targets matched to complete local-day ERA5 weather. Across seven rolling monthly holdouts, weather improves XGBoost RMSE in 6 of 7 folds and LightGBM in 5 of 7; pooled RMSE falls about 37% for both model families. A Jan–Mar XGBoost ablation finds temperature-only features reduce RMSE 48.1% versus calendar-only, while removing temperature eliminates the weather gain.

Retrospective same-day reanalysis; one financial year; not causal or day-ahead forecasting evidence.

Hydro flexibility

In the reference 4,455 MW transfer case, moving the same daily hydro energy within the day reduces modeled unserved energy from about 375.03 GWh to 11.75 GWh. This is an upper-bound timing sensitivity, not a claim about physically achievable reservoir operation.

External transfer

The model uses the CEA 4,455 MW ATC snapshot as a dated reference and transparent 80%/60% stress cases. The result is a sensitivity to dependable transfer, not a forecast of future ATC.

Internal deliverability

Twenty-one source-backed lines remain overloaded across all eight tested boundary-allocation cases, forming twelve robust corridor groups. The strongest Shoranur-area signal is independently corroborated by current CEA planning evidence, while modeled loading magnitudes remain screening-only.

Data acquisition

The next scientific upgrade is operational data, not more speculative model breadth.

Priority A

Aggregated Kerala 15-minute/hourly demand for one or more complete years.

Priority B

State internal generation by interval, ideally plant-wise hydro plus non-hydro totals.

Priority C

Scheduled and actual interstate drawal/import by interval, plus system-level restriction timestamps where available.

Priority D

Substation/transformer loading and current network equipment parameters for stronger spatial validation.

Fallback if interval load is unavailable

Generate an ensemble of plausible hourly chronologies consistent with observed daily energy, published extrema and aggregate constraints, then propagate that uncertainty through the adequacy results.

Optional later case study

A 2026 stress-event reconstruction belongs after the general model is frozen and only if public operational data are sufficient to separate physical constraints from operational choices.
