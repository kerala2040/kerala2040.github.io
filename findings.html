RQ1 · Historical balance

Demand has grown; external electricity remains structurally important.

Boundary rule

No invented “import share”.

Official annual consumption, category sales, periphery input and reported import-energy totals are not divided into one percentage when definitions differ.

Inference: Kerala's system cannot be analysed as a self-contained generation island. External electricity is part of the normal balance, but the exact dependence metric depends on accounting boundary and timescale.

Shoranur: corrected interpretation

The model's historical 2023 transformer snapshot included a 100 MVA Shornur element. Current 2026 CEA material reports 200+100 MVA existing transformation and independently identifies Shoranur as constrained. The location is corroborated; the old 100 MVA overload magnitude is not current whole-station loading.

This is linear congestion/deliverability screening. It does not establish AC voltage stability, transient stability, frequency stability or N-1 security.

Synthesis

No single “root cause” survives the evidence as the whole story.

Conference-safe conclusion: Under the current public-data evidence, Kerala's susceptibility to electricity stress is best treated as an interaction among demand timing and weather, hydropower timing/availability, dependable interstate transfer and internal network deliverability. The relative causal contribution of those mechanisms to any specific restriction event is not identified.
