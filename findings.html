Results / independent research record

Findings supported by the admitted evidence

Results are restricted to historical source-reported quantities and a within-year retrospective modelling experiment using observed daily demand.

Annual electricity-consumption series

State Planning Board consumer-side consumption increased from 22,540.32 MU in FY2020–21 to 29,311.76 MU in FY2024–25, a change of approximately 30.04% within the publisher’s reported annual series.

Source: Economic Review 2025, Volume I, pp. 568–569; derived calculation and accounting notes.

Availability and consistency of daily SLDC records

The FY2024–25 source archive contains 354 observed dated reports out of 365 days. Eleven source days were not available in the archived input. The audit of observed daily accounting reports a maximum absolute numerical balance residual of 0.0001 MU.

The observed-day SLDC consumption-field sum is 30,666.2569 MU; its operational accounting boundary differs from the State Planning Board’s consumer-side annual consumption statistic.

Source: FY2024–25 SLDC QA manifest; SLDC source reports.

The analysed dataset contains 353 observed daily-consumption targets with matching complete India-local ERA5 days. Both model families were independently tuned for calendar-only and weather-augmented variants across seven chronological monthly holdouts. The November 2024 holdout showed no improvement for either model family.

ERA5 inputs describe weather realised on the same day as the demand target. These error reductions quantify a retrospective association in one financial year, not day-ahead forecast performance or a causal temperature effect.

Source: ML robustness report and fold-level outcomes; machine-readable result summary.

Not adopted as findings in this release

Shortage MWh/GWh from reconstructed-hour scenarios, assumed hydro ramping, future transfer limits, network overload maps, hypothetical storage deployment and 2040 investment portfolios are retained as reproducible exploratory work. They are not presented as measured shortages, validated physical constraints or planning recommendations. Exploratory source list.
