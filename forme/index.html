Memorize this first

The core story in 30 seconds.

30-second answer: “Kerala2040 is a public-data diagnostic of Kerala's electricity system. I reconstructed the historical balance and a bounded hourly chronology, then tested whether stress is driven by difficult-hour demand, hydropower timing, dependence on interstate transfer and internal transmission deliverability. I treat ecology and finance as feasibility constraints and use storage/flexibility cases only as candidate response mechanisms. The result is not a final 2040 plan; it is a structured diagnosis of which mechanisms matter and which evidence is still missing.”
One-sentence contribution: “The contribution is a source-audited evidence chain that separates observation, reconstruction, sensitivity and validation, and keeps higher-confidence claims fail-closed when the public data cannot support them.”
Main research question: “Why is Kerala susceptible to electricity shortages and peak-hour supply restrictions, and what structural and operational factors determine whether the system can meet demand under stress?”
Working synthesis: “Kerala's susceptibility to stress is an interaction problem rather than a simple annual-energy deficit: demand timing and weather, hydro timing and availability, dependable external transfer and internal network deliverability can each become binding under different conditions.”

RQ1–RQ6

Know what each question is doing.

RQ1 · Historical balance

How has Kerala's electricity balance changed, and how structurally important is electricity arriving from outside the state?

Evidence: annual official statistics + SLDC daily archive. Key boundary: do not collapse different import/consumption definitions into one percentage.

RQ2 · Energy vs chronology

Is the problem mainly annual energy shortage, or difficult-hour timing/flexibility?

Evidence: reconstructed 8,760-hour demand, ERA5 shape terms, chronological adequacy. Key result: annual energy alone cannot describe stressed hours.

RQ3 · Hydro flexibility

How much adequacy value comes from being able to move hydro energy in time?

Evidence: v1.1 same-day timing + v1.2 interday windows. Key result: very large benefit when timing-limited; much smaller under deep transfer/energy deficit.

RQ4 · Interstate transfer

How sensitive is Kerala to dependable external supply and transfer capability?

Evidence: CEA 4,455 MW ATC reference + 80%/60% synthetic stress cases. Key result: adequacy deteriorates sharply under strong transfer constraints.

RQ5 · Internal deliverability

Can statewide balance appear adequate while internal corridors remain stressed?

Evidence: public 110-kV+ multibus screen under eight import-allocation cases. Key result: persistent Shoranur-area geographic signal, externally corroborated by CEA.

RQ6 · Response classes

Given the diagnosed vulnerabilities, which interventions deserve deeper modelling?

Candidate classes: better use of existing hydro/demand flexibility → storage/reinforcement → new supply. Not: an optimized policy portfolio.

Methodology logic

Why each method was chosen.

Why source/accounting reconstruction first?

Because capacity-expansion precision is meaningless if historical inputs mix different boundaries. I preserved definitions and missingness before optimization.

Why not reconcile all annual numbers?

Because broad consumption, category sales, periphery input and SLDC balance use different definitions. Reconciliation without source-supported adjustments would invent data.

Why 8,760 hours?

Peak adequacy, renewable coincidence and storage depend on chronology. Representative periods can hide rare stressful sequences.

Why FY2024–25?

It has the strongest near-complete daily SLDC evidence bundle and aligns well with current planning baselines. It is not claimed to be a climatological design year.

Why PyPSA?

Open, transparent, chronological and reproducible. The limiting factor was evidence, not solver capability.

Why cross-check with SciPy/OSeMOSYS?

To reduce framework-implementation risk. Agreement verifies the common mathematical formulation, not physical reality.

Why adequacy-first before cost?

No Kerala-specific VOLL was admitted. A monetary objective with invented VOLL would choose how much load shedding is “worth it.” I first minimize unserved energy, then cost among equally adequate solutions.

Why no unit commitment?

Plant-level minimum output, ramping, startup, heat-rate and outage data are incomplete. Guessed parameters would create false precision.

Why no probabilistic LOLP?

Proper LOLP requires outage distributions, multiple weather years, hydro uncertainty and demand uncertainty. I report deterministic unserved MWh and shortage hours.

Why no AC / N-1 network model?

Missing measured bus injections, full impedances, reactive-power data and current operating states. An AC solver can converge with bad inputs; convergence is not validation.

Equations to understand

You do not need to derive everything at the poster, but know what each equation means.

Daily-energy conservation

Σₕ Load(d,h) = E_obs(d)

The 24 reconstructed hourly values must sum to the observed SLDC daily MWh on qualified days. Therefore daily energy is data; intraday shape is the modeled part.

Lexicographic adequacy

Stage 1: min Σₜ Uₜ
Stage 2: hold U* and min partial cost

U is unserved energy. This avoids inventing a Kerala-specific VOLL that would decide how much load shedding the optimizer tolerates.

Idukki v1.3 reconstructed forcing

Bₜ = Sₜ₊₁ − Sₜ + Gₜ/c

B is reconstructed net water-equivalent balance, S storage, G generation and c the fixed station energy equivalent. Because B uses S and G, historical closure is algebraic.

Idukki state transition

Sₜ₊₁ = Sₜ + Bₜ − Gₜ/c

This makes v1.3 stateful, but it does not turn reconstructed B into measured catchment inflow.

Gross Idukki source ratio

2,190,000 MWh / 1,460 MCM = 1,500 MWh/MCM

The model uses 1,470 MWh/MCM instead because it derives the median station-capability/effective-storage ratio across the retained source rows.

Transmission thermal apparent-power screen

S ≈ √3 · V · I

Used where a source current rating and nominal voltage define a screening MVA. It is not an operator seasonal/emergency rating.

Demand / ERA5

Questions you are very likely to get.

You don't have hourly telemetry. How can you model 8,760 hours?

“It is a reconstruction, not telemetry. I conserve the observed daily MWh exactly on 354 days and reconstruct only the intraday shape using reported extrema, clock time, weekend structure and ERA5 weather.”

What does 124.9 MW MAE mean?

“It is the MAE on held-out reported intraday extrema, conditional on knowing that day's total energy. It validates shape reconstruction, not a day-ahead hourly load forecast.”

Is daily-energy normalization leakage?

“Only if I claimed to forecast daily demand. I don't. Daily energy is intentionally an observed constraint; the validation target is intraday shape.”

Why ERA5?

“It provides complete, physically consistent and reproducible hourly weather chronology. It is reanalysis, not local station measurement.”

Do weather coefficients prove causation?

“No. They are associational shape terms. Weather, season, behavior and clock time are correlated, and the series is daily-normalized.”

Why not LSTM/XGBoost?

“I do not have a dense measured hourly target series. Complex supervised forecasting without hourly labels would be pseudo-sophistication.”

Why are 5,904 and 5,797 MW both present?

“They come from different CEA planning/reporting contexts. 5,904 MW is the generation-RA reference used by the proxy; 5,797 MW is the recorded FY2024–25 peak in the newer transmission-RA plan. I keep them separate.”

Can the v2 fit be regenerated?

“The released 8,760-hour payload is hash-pinned and downstream use is reproducible, but the original fitting script and raw ERA5 archive are not currently committed, so the coefficient fit itself is not fully source-to-fit reproducible from a fresh clone.”

Hydro / Idukki

Keep sensitivity, reservoir physics and hydrology separate.

96.9% reduction sounds too large.

“It is large and case-specific. The experiment holds daily hydro energy fixed and changes only its timing. In the high-transfer reference case shortage is mostly a timing problem. Under tighter transfer the benefit collapses—that contrast is the result.”

Does v1.1 represent actual KSEB dispatch freedom?

“No. It is an upper-bound timing sensitivity. It omits reservoir inflow, cascade routing, minimum releases, rule curves and head-dependent efficiency.”

What are 1/3/15/30-day windows?

“Synthetic temporal brackets that test how added interday freedom changes adequacy. They are not reservoir durations.”

Is v1.3 a validated reservoir model?

“No. It is a stateful pilot with reconstructed net-water-balance forcing.”

But historical storage closes exactly.

“That closure is algebraic by construction because the forcing is derived from the same storage change and generation. It validates implementation and alignment, not hydrology.”

What is 1,470 MWh/MCM?

“A source-derived station energy-equivalent ratio from reported generation capability and effective storage. It is not a first-principles head/efficiency curve.”

Why isn't Phase 5 catchment finished?

“Candidate reconstructions did not pass independent geometry checks. I refused to tune a polygon just to match the 649.3 km² reference area.”

Phase 4 rainfall-inflow: forecast?

“No. Same-day rainfall is used, so the 37.24% held-out MAE improvement is a retrospective diagnostic on complete cases, not a one-day-ahead forecast.”

Imports / transfer

Do not turn interconnection into an ideological claim.

Are imports a weakness?

“Not inherently. Interconnection is normal and valuable. The vulnerability is dependence on external supply that may not be available at the required hour or through the required transfer path. The issue is dependable access, not autarky.”

Why 4,455 MW?

“CEA reports current TTC 4,575 MW and ATC 4,455 MW in the dated Kerala transmission plan. I use 4,455 as a current screening reference, not a guaranteed future capability.”

Why 80% and 60%?

“They are transparent stress cases to test sensitivity to external-transfer availability. They are not observed outage states or predictions.”

Why not calculate future ATC from planned line MVA?

“ATC is a simultaneous system property constrained by topology, contingencies, voltage, thermal limits and operating point. You cannot add line ratings arithmetically.”

Are the import costs hourly landed prices?

“No. They are flat source-derived benchmarks from different accounting boundaries, which is why the economics are partial.”

What exactly is ₹5.24/kWh?

“The FY2023–24 IEX average MCP, rounded from ₹5.23759/kWh. The volume-weighted MCP is ₹5.17449/kWh, so I do not call ₹5.24 weighted.”

Transmission

The geography is stronger than the exact loading magnitude.

Why “network screen” and not “grid model”?

“I have source-backed topology and partial equipment evidence but not measured bus loads, unit dispatch, full source impedances, reactive power or N-1 states.”

Why generic X?

“Conductor identity does not uniquely determine positive-sequence reactance. Where source X was unavailable I used explicit voltage-class screening assumptions.”

Why load ∝ transformer MVA?

“No public hourly bus-load series exists. Distribution-interface transformer MVA is an electrically grounded proxy for connection scale; it is still only a proxy.”

What does 21 robust lines mean?

“The same 21 source-rating-backed lines exceed their screening rating in every one of eight boundary-injection allocation cases. Robust only to that uncertainty dimension.”

Did you discover Shoranur independently?

“The public screen independently highlighted the geography. QA then found the old transformer magnitude used a 2023 100 MVA snapshot, while current CEA reports 200+100 MVA and independently identifies Shoranur and nearby corridors as constrained. I retain the location, not the old current-loading interpretation.”

Does the Shoranur correction invalidate the network study?

“It downgrades one transformer magnitude. It does not erase the topology or line-screen signal, and current CEA independently corroborates several hotspot locations.”

Can you say grid stability?

“No. I say network stress, congestion or deliverability screening. No transient, small-signal, frequency or voltage-stability analysis was performed.”

Can you size upgrades from overload MW?

“No. Required reinforcement depends on future topology, redispatch, generation/load changes and contingency criteria.”

Economics / finance / budgets

Always state the price boundary and project stage.

Is this a least-cost plan?

“No. Existing fleet economics and several system costs are omitted. It is a partial economic expansion screen.”

Are CEA costs Kerala project quotes?

“No. They are national planning assumptions at a 2021–22 cost basis.”

Is 9.08% Kerala WACC?

“No. It is CERC's non-SHP post-tax WACC-equivalent regulatory benchmark used for research annualization.”

Are Kerala projects unusually cheap?

“I do not claim that. Some planning estimates look modest in SEK, but scope and project stage differ and overruns exist. The defensible point is that capital constraints make prioritization important.”

What are the PSP budget figures?

“₹180 crore for 30 MW Manjappara and ₹573 crore for 100 MW Mudirapuzha are early planning estimates, not final EPC costs.”

Why convert to SEK?

“Only to give the Swedish conference audience intuitive scale. INR remains authoritative and the FX date/project stage must be visible.”

Solutions / technology questions

Candidate mechanisms, not recommendations.

Does storage save electricity?

“No. It normally loses energy. It reduces stress by moving usable energy between hours.”

Is pumped storage cheap?

“Not categorically. Civil works, tunnels, ecology, geology and grid connection are site-specific.”

What do EV/TES/industry pilots prove?

“Only that the mechanism can shift authored service away from stressed hours. They do not establish statewide MW.”

Why not nuclear / SMRs?

“I did not exclude them ideologically. A credible Kerala nuclear scenario needs project-specific cost, construction time, siting, cooling, federal/regulatory ownership, unit size and grid assumptions. Adding a generic SMR variable without those would be invented precision.”

Could nuclear still be useful?

“Yes, firm low-carbon generation is a mechanism worth comparing in a properly defined long-term scenario. This study does not establish whether it is or is not the best Kerala option.”

Why not coal/gas?

“Same principle: serious comparison requires fuel logistics, emissions/policy, project costs and operating assumptions. The diagnosis does not need to preselect a technology.”

What is the response hierarchy?

“First improve use of existing assets and flexible demand; then add storage/reinforcement; then add new supply where the diagnosed constraint and feasibility evidence justify it.”

Are these policy recommendations?

“No. They are candidate response classes motivated by the diagnostic evidence and need deeper technical, economic, ecological and reliability modelling.”

Likely high-level questions

Short, clean answers.

What is the biggest limitation?

“Lack of synchronized operator-grade interval and spatial data. That forces hourly demand and bus allocation to be reconstructed.”

What would falsify your synthesis?

“Measured events showing restrictions while dependable generation, external transfer and internal network headroom were all ample would mean my mechanism set is incomplete. Calibrated network evidence contradicting the hotspot geography would also force revision.”

Why should I trust a screening model?

“Only for the question it is designed to answer. Sensitivities can isolate mechanisms and prioritize where better evidence is worth acquiring; they are not substitutes for operator-grade validation.”

Was this peer reviewed?

“No. It is independent research presented for technical discussion. That is why the repository publishes the evidence classes, assumptions, limitations and QA corrections.”

Did AI build this?

“AI tools assisted with coding, source discovery, QA and documentation. AI output is not used as evidence; the evidence layer is the primary documents, retained data, explicit transformations and reproducible tests. I remain responsible for every claim.”

Why compare Kerala with Sweden?

“Only to give the CET audience scale intuition. It is not a development or performance ranking; climate, heating, industry and electricity systems differ substantially.”

Why include Kerala culture/geography?

“Because geography is part of the engineering boundary: mountains, monsoon, dense settlement, forests, coast and backwaters affect hydro, hazards, land availability and transmission. Cultural context simply makes the place legible to an international audience.”

So why did the 2026 load shedding happen?

“I cannot reconstruct the exact event cause from the admitted public data. I would need interval dispatch, unit outages, interface schedules/flows, market availability, reservoir constraints and internal telemetry. The event motivates the study; the model tests structural vulnerabilities.”

Wording traps

Never say these casually.

Do not say: “The model proves why the 2026 load shedding happened.”Say: “The study tests structural vulnerabilities that can make the system susceptible to stress; it does not reconstruct the exact event.”
Do not say: “Grid stability.”Say: “Network congestion/deliverability screening” or “power-system stress.”
Do not say: “Hourly forecast.”Say: “Weather-sensitive hourly reconstruction conditional on observed daily energy.”
Do not say: “OSeMOSYS validates PyPSA.”Say: “Cross-framework agreement verifies implementation equivalence under shared assumptions.”
Do not say: “v1.3 validates Idukki hydrology.”Say: “v1.3 is a stateful pilot with algebraic historical closure under reconstructed forcing.”
Do not say: “Shoranur is a 100 MVA station.”Say: “The old model used a 2023 100 MVA element; current CEA reports 200+100 MVA existing transformation and still identifies the location as constrained.”
Do not say: “21 proven congested lines.”Say: “21 source-rating-backed lines exceed screening rating in all eight tested boundary-injection allocations.”
Do not say: “17 MWp installed at Water Metro.”Say: “The annual report envisages about 17 MWp capable of meeting its energy needs.”
Do not say: “5.389 MWp is Kochi Metro's total current solar capacity.”Say: “It is a documented project-stage configuration; later deployment expanded.”
Do not say: “Buildable renewable potential.”Say: “Research scenario envelope; statutory siting stack remains incomplete.”
Do not say: “Optimal 2040 solution / least-cost plan.”Say: “Partial economic screening and candidate response classes.”
Do not say: “Pumped storage is cheap” or “storage saves electricity.”Say: “Storage shifts energy with losses; PSP economics/feasibility are site-specific.”
Do not say: “Kerala builds cheaply and high-quality.”Say: “Capital-constrained/budget-conscious planning makes prioritization important.”
Do not say: “Every result is reproducible from a fresh clone.”Say: “Most retained transformations/model runs are reproducible; the ERA5-sensitive v2 fit is integrity-verifiable but the original fitter/raw archive are not public in the repo.”

15-minute pre-poster drill

Read this immediately before the session.

  1. Say the question: susceptibility to shortages / peak-hour restrictions; structural + operational factors.
  2. Say the contribution: source-audited evidence chain, not new solver.
  3. Know the four mechanisms: demand/weather · hydro timing · external transfer · internal deliverability.
  4. Know the data boundary: 354 observed FY24–25 days; 11 missing; hourly chronology reconstructed.
  5. Know the load metric caveat: 124.9 MW MAE is held-out intraday extrema conditional on known daily MWh.
  6. Know the transfer reference: TTC 4575 / ATC 4455 MW; 3564/2673 are synthetic stress cases.
  7. Know the hydro result: 375.03 → 11.75 GWh at full transfer/reference demand; upper-bound timing sensitivity.
  8. Know Idukki: 780 MW, 1460 MCM, 1470 MWh/MCM operational equivalent; v1.3 closure is algebraic.
  9. Know network: 233 primary lines, 21 robust to eight import-allocation cases, 12 corridor groups.
  10. Know Shoranur correction: old 100 MVA element vs current CEA 200+100 MVA; keep location, drop old current-loading interpretation.
  11. Know what is not done: no AC/N-1, LOLP, event reconstruction, validated catchment, buildable GW or least-cost portfolio.
  12. If uncertain: say “I don't have evidence to answer that at the required boundary; the data I would need are …” Do not improvise.
Best closing sentence: “The central result is not that Kerala needs one specific technology. It is that timing, external transfer and internal deliverability can become binding for different reasons, so the next investment should be chosen against the diagnosed constraint rather than against an annual energy headline.”
