What the QA changed

The audit corrected interpretation, not just formatting.

Source vintage

Shoranur transformer

The public model used a genuine 2023 100 MVA transformer element. Current 2026 CEA planning reports 200+100 MVA existing transformation. The hotspot geography remains independently corroborated; the old overload magnitude is not current whole-station loading.

Circularity

Idukki v1.3 storage replay

Historical storage closure is algebraic because the forcing is reconstructed from storage change and generation. It verifies implementation, not hydrology.

Validation scope

ERA5 demand reconstruction

The held-out test evaluates intraday shape conditional on known daily energy. It is not an hourly demand forecast.

Robustness scope

21 network lines

“Robust” means the same lines exceed screening rating across eight tested residual-import injection allocations—not across every load, dispatch, impedance or contingency uncertainty.

Price statistic

IEX ₹5.24/kWh

This is the FY2023–24 average MCP (~₹5.23759/kWh), not the volume-weighted MCP (~₹5.17449/kWh).

Source context

5,904 vs 5,797 MW

The first is the CEA generation-RA planning reference used by the load proxy; the second is the separately reported FY2024–25 recorded peak in the newer transmission-RA plan.

Supported conclusions

What can be stated confidently within the study boundary.

Official

Demand growth is real.

Broad electricity consumption rose from 22.54 to 29.31 TWh between FY2020–21 and FY2024–25.

Chronological screen

Timing matters.

Renewable spill and shortage can coexist within the same screening year; annual energy alone cannot establish adequacy.

Sensitivity

Hydro flexibility matters.

The same hydro energy can have very different adequacy value depending on timing and transfer conditions.

Official

4,455 MW ATC is a valid dated reference.

CEA provides this current Kerala transfer-capability figure; the model does not treat it as a guaranteed future capability.

Independent corroboration

Shoranur is a defensible hotspot location.

The public screen and current CEA planning independently point to the same geography.

Methodological

Fail-closed gates are part of the result.

Unverified catchment geometry, incomplete statutory siting layers and unsupported future ATC are not promoted to stronger claims.

Scope boundary

What this study does not establish.

Not demonstrated here

  • Exact operational cause of a specific 2026 load-shedding event.
  • Measured hourly demand telemetry or day-ahead hourly forecast skill.
  • Transient, frequency, voltage or small-signal “grid stability”.
  • AC/N-1 security or operator-validated congestion.
  • Hydrologically validated Idukki catchment/cascade operation.
  • Statutory buildable renewable capacity.
  • Probabilistic LOLP/ELCC.
  • A total-system least-cost 2040 portfolio.

What the study does provide

  • Source-audited historical electricity evidence.
  • A bounded hourly demand reconstruction.
  • Chronological adequacy and transfer sensitivities.
  • Hydro timing and stateful pilot experiments.
  • Public-data transmission stress screening.
  • Independent implementation checks.
  • Candidate response mechanisms and evidence gaps.

Reproducibility

Most released model runs are reproducible; one fitting stage remains incomplete.

Reproducible layers

Historical transformations, PyPSA screens, hydro timing experiments, cross-solver comparisons, network screens and synthetic flexibility pilots retain code/config/evidence sufficient to reproduce released calculations subject to source access.

ERA5-sensitive v2 fit

The released 8,760-hour payload, hashes, metadata and downstream use are reproducible. The original fitting script and raw ERA5 archive were not committed, so the coefficient fit cannot currently be regenerated from a fresh public clone.
